## Important Notice on Contribution Architecture

This Contributor License Agreement ("CLA") represents a **fallback licensing framework** and is **not** the project owner's preferred path:

1. **Retention of Copyright (No Transfer):** Under this CLA, the Contributor retains copyright ownership in their Contribution and merely grants a broad license to the Project Owner.
2. **CLA Does Not Unify Ownership:** A CLA does **not** transfer copyright and does **not** make the Project Owner the sole copyright owner. Consequently, copyright remains fragmented among individual contributors.
3. **Contrast with Copyright Assignment Agreement (CAA):** For NyxWin, the **preferred approach** is the [Copyright Assignment Agreement (CAA)](CAA.md) with a non-commercial license-back. The CAA unifies copyright ownership under the Project Owner while granting contributors a personal, royalty-free non-commercial license to their own code. The CAA and CLA are fundamentally different legal instruments and are **not** equivalent.

## Clauses Requiring Qualified Legal Review

Before this agreement can be approved for execution, qualified legal counsel must review and adapt:

1. **Scope of Non-Exclusive Commercial Sublicensing:** Whether a non-exclusive license granting unilateral commercial sublicensing rights provides adequate protection to commercial downstream licensees in all target jurisdictions without copyright assignment.
2. **Patent Grant and Defensive Retaliation:** Enforceability and scope of the patent grant, and whether defensive termination provisions should be incorporated.
3. **Employer Rights and Labor Law:** The sufficiency of individual employee warranties regarding employer intellectual property across differing international labor regimes.
4. **Enforceability of Moral Rights Non-Assertion:** Whether a non-assertion agreement effectively shields the Project Owner and commercial licensees in civil law countries where moral rights cannot be waived.
5. **Governing Law and Dispute Resolution:** Designating appropriate governing law, venue, and enforcement mechanisms once the contracting entity is established.
